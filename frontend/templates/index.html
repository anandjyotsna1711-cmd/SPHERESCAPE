The colours aren't real, because infrared has no colour we can see: brighter signal is drawn brighter. Brightness is in MJy/sr, the unit astronomers use for how bright a patch of sky is. Pixel (0, 0) is at the bottom-left. "Circle detected stars" marks the 2,000 strongest of our detections.

HOW SHARP IS SPHEREx'S VISION ACROSS THE DETECTOR?

No telescope shows a star as a perfect dot. It spreads each star into a tiny blur called the point spread function. SPHEREx's image files include that blur measured in 121 zones across the detector. Each zone also sees a slightly different wavelength of infrared light. Hover over or tap a zone to see its numbers.

The detector, 2,040 × 2,040 pixels. Top-left is pixel (0, 0).
FINDING STARS IN ONE IMAGE

We wrote a detector that removes the sky's background glow, measures the noise, and keeps only clear points of light. Then we checked every detection against 2MASS, an earlier infrared survey that catalogued hundreds of millions of stars at almost the same wavelengths. We used it as an answer key.
